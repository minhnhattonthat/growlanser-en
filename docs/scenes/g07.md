- **Where and who:** The Magic Academy, including its cafeteria, classrooms, libraries, laboratories, offices, and roof. Named speakers include Tippi, Misha, Luise, Ariost, Headmaster Maxwell, and Kokko the chicken, alongside students, professors, shop staff, a librarian, and Maxwell’s secretary.
- **Hot-spring trip:** Misha asks to accompany the tournament-winning party to Comsprings: their travel voucher covers five people. She identifies Maxwell as her guardian. Luise wants another girl’s company at the baths; even the refusal branch leads to companions supporting Misha’s inclusion. Misha joins, expressing enthusiastic affection for the hero.
- **Academy life:** Students complain about landslides, examinations, homesickness, teaching, and the war between Rolandia and Burnstein. Misha’s collisions and untidiness produce comic exchanges. Students discuss Ariost’s completed flying apparatus and wish he could invent something to stop the war.

Other texts cover the unusually powerful crystal deposit, the unexplained disappearance of Growsians during the war a century earlier, Growsian and Featherian ruins, and a forest-disorientation device whose sealed entrance is controlled from Fort Rajin.
- **Invisibility potion:** A professor needs Clearano grass from Mount Bream. If supplied, he makes a potion, tests it successfully on Kokko, and gives the party some. He warns that its unstable effect will not last long.
- **Permits and investigation:** Maxwell supplies the Comsprings pass and requires its return. Separately, he authorizes entry to the crystal mine and invokes an international magical-technology law while requesting an investigation into suspected crystal smuggling. Acceptance can bring a Mage Staff, or 500 Elm if it cannot be carried.

## s031 - Searching the Academy Laboratories

- **Where and who:** The Magic Academy, especially Maxwell’s and Bradley’s basement laboratories. Speakers include Tippi, Luise, Ariost, Misha, Wallace, Maxwell, Bradley, their secretaries, and unnamed students and staff. Sandra is discussed but does not speak here.
- **Disappearance and secrecy:** Students discuss the disappearance of the Growsians who were in the hall. Maxwell says he and Bradley want the incident kept quiet to protect the academy’s reputation while they search. He accepts that the party will continue its own investigation.
- **Securing access:** The party wants to inspect the basement laboratories, mentioning boxes brought into the academy. Maxwell readily agrees to show his research. Bradley initially refuses, but Wallace explicitly names him as a suspect and pressures him into permitting an inspection.
- **Maxwell’s research:** Maxwell studies why Growsians possess exceptional magic and whether ordinary people can acquire it. His proposed connection to the original world remains a hypothesis. He explains that Grosh amplifies ordinary magic and credits Sandra’s discovery of Grosh in magic crystals; Luise identifies this as her mother’s research. Residual Grosh extracted from crystals cannot grant Growsian abilities.
- **Bradley’s research:** His laboratory yields nothing visibly suspicious. He explains that he classifies curses and develops ways to remove them, enabling exploration of previously inaccessible Growsian ruins. Curses differ from ordinary spells through persistence and reach; longer, broader effects require longer rituals.
- **Competing suspicions:** Neither inspection establishes a culprit. Misha defends Maxwell, while Wallace finds his apparent innocence suspicious. The party notes that both researchers need Growsians for their work. Player choices allow suspicion of Maxwell, Bradley, or someone else; the earring clue remains relevant.
- **Possible surveillance:** Wallace feels someone anticipates their actions. He uses Tippi’s ability to communicate remotely with Sandra as an example of how this could happen, explicitly avoiding a direct accusation. He stops Tippi from contacting her remotely, and the party proposes visiting Sandra in person.
- **Type and tone:** Primarily a main-story investigation, surrounded by recurring academy dialogue, library texts, potion acquisition, and navigation menus. The tone combines suspicion, guarded questioning, scientific exposition, and comic friction. The supplied text leaves responsibility unresolved and contains no clear debug or battle sequence.
