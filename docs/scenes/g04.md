## s018 - Debug Menus and Game Show Skit

- **Type and setting:** Predominantly debug/system text, covering locations and events throughout the game. It also contains Dungeon Man’s labyrinth introduction and a promotional skit explicitly set at a game show.
- **Speakers:** Dungeon Man introduces his maze; Tippi and Misha speak in the promotional skit, addressing the player-named hero. Most other character names appear as menu selections or portrait labels, rather than speakers in a continuous scene.
- Debug options grant Luise magic, equip the party, disable encounters, change parameters and names, select companions, and jump to dungeons, battles, story checkpoints, and vacation events. Technical instructions warn that jumping between story periods can disrupt flags.
- Story-testing menus reference rescuing Leticia and Irene, an alliance with Ranzak, Elliot’s recovery of the throne, battles against Gevel, and the subsequent pursuit of Wenzel. These are selectable checkpoints, not events enacted consecutively here.
- Relationship and outcome switches include Karen’s rescue, hospitalization, death, or availability; Misha’s flower crown and consolation scene; Julian’s duel, family-related conversation, and dress appearance; and success with individual companions. Portrait tests likewise list emotional, injured, disguised, and transformed states.
- In the game show skit, Misha arrives late after fixing her makeup and falling over. Tippi mocks her, while Misha boasts that a man called her cute, then professes devotion to the hero and imagines a romantic embrace beside Makuhari’s sea breeze and sunset.
- **Tone:** Functional developer shorthand mixed with jokes and unfinished labels. The closing skit is playful, openly promotional comedy: Tippi interrupts Misha’s fantasy with a kick and asks the audience to support *Growlanser*.

## s019 - Sandra’s Laboratory: Counsel and Betrayal

- **Type and setting:** Main-story material spanning several visits to **Sandra’s Magic Research Institute**, including a rescue confrontation and later investigation updates. The file is not one chronological conversation.
- **Speakers and relationships:** Sandra, Luise, Tippi, Wallace, Misha, Wenzel, and other party members speak. Luise calls Sandra her mother and the hero her older brother; Tippi calls Sandra “Master.” Sandra addresses Wenzel as her teacher.
- Wallace privately raises the possibility that the party is being watched through Tippi. Sandra explains that a sufficiently skilled magician could spy through a homunculus and supplies a **Magic Jammer**. It blocks interception but also prevents telepathic communication between Tippi and Sandra; Tippi feels uneasy and mentally foggy.
- Sandra gives Luise her magic-practicum completion certificate, to be delivered to the Academy’s headmaster or vice-headmaster; only graduation research remains afterward. Misha feels left behind academically but resolves to stay near Luise and learn from her.
- Sandra questions the crystal mine’s supposedly sound ceiling: investigations more than a decade apart both suffered collapses immediately after entering. The current headmaster alone participated in both, making deliberate interference a possibility, not a proven conclusion. Separately, she recommends investigating the village northeast of Garuaos Prison, near where Wallace lost his eye and arm.
- Later consultations concern Gevel’s extraordinary regeneration, Richard’s service to Wenzel and possible cooperation, Irene’s restoration, and the hero and Richard’s ability together to turn people into Growsians. Power Stones are described as absorbing the backlash from space-time control and as something only Growsians can create. Sandra also worries about the hero’s health and the difficulty of tracking a teleporting Wenzel.
- Under the pretext of comparing Luise’s Grosh with spent Grosh in magic crystals, Wenzel has her wear a device and imagine using magic. He instead extracts her pure Grosh, boasting that the amount will restore his true power. Sandra and the party react in horror and prepare to take Luise home.
- Another sequence depicts **Sandra’s rescue**: Luise unexpectedly teleports the party to the laboratory, where an attacker threatens Sandra and largely resists her magic. Afterward, Sandra explains that eclipse-born Growsians possess special magic and that Luise, born during a total solar eclipse, has the strongest category of potential. **Tone:** Warm family concern and teasing alternate with investigation, danger, betrayal, and urgent encouragement.

## s020 - Fireworks Interrupted

- **Type and setting:** A companion-focused fireworks event atop **Sandra’s tower**, with alternate character branches, followed by a main-story threat. Tippi has obtained Sandra’s permission to use the tower.
- **Speakers:** Tippi, Luise, Misha, Karen, Julian, Wallace, Ariost, Elliot, Xenos, and Wenzel, with player response choices for the hero. The companions arrive early, wait for nightfall, and wake the sleeping hero when the fireworks begin.
- In Tippi’s more serious branch, her first sight of fireworks prompts her to discuss her limited lifespan as a homunculus. Her assigned role was to monitor the hero, which will end once Wenzel is defeated; she wishes she could remain with him longer. She says Misha can live a long life, unlike herself, but stresses that she is not about to die immediately.
- Luise recalls watching fireworks from the roof with the hero as children, nearly falling, and getting roof-climbing forbidden by their mother. Misha reflects that, despite the shock of learning she is not human, being created allowed her to meet the hero and experience happiness; another exchange has her drift into a romantic fantasy.
- Karen remembers watching fireworks with her brother; one branch emphasizes the comfort of having the hero within reach, while another anticipates going with her brother again. Julian has both restrained and intimate dialogue: the affectionate branch uses “My Lord,” offers embrace or closeness choices, and expresses a wish to keep feeling the hero’s warmth.
- Wallace sees only flashes but enjoys the powerful sound and renewed public spirit. Ariost, Elliot, and Xenos likewise take heart from the celebration and express confidence or determination about defeating Wenzel.
- Wenzel interrupts with a public declaration that he can travel instantly wherever he wishes. He threatens to destroy the **Magic Academy at noon the next day** as an example. Through Tippi, Sandra instructs the party to wait at home overnight and assemble before the king in the morning.
- **Tone:** Wonder, nostalgia, affection, and occasional comedy, with bittersweet revelations in some branches; the ending abruptly turns threatening and urgent.

## s021 - Delis Village and Wallace’s Recruitment

- **Type and setting:** **Delis Village**, including its streets, shops, and inn. The file combines main-story arrival and recruitment, a Julian character event, changing village dialogue, shop interfaces, and a save-point tutorial.
- **Speakers:** Tippi, Luise, Wallace, Julian, other party members, and unnamed villagers, merchants, inn staff, a village chief, a child, a martial artist, and a wandering traveler. Nolan is mentioned as someone who formerly taught a child swordsmanship here.
- The hero, Luise, and Tippi arrive to meet Wallace at the inn on Sandra’s behalf. Since he is expected in the evening, they separate briefly to explore. When he arrives, the party delivers Sandra’s **Magic Eye**, with alternate responses if the hero delays handing it over.
- Wallace explains that the eye lets him distinguish vague human silhouettes, a major improvement over seeing nothing. Hearing that Sandra’s research book has been stolen, he reports a woodcutter’s sighting of suspicious people at a cabin in the eastern forest and joins the recovery expedition to protect Sandra’s children and practice using his artificial eye and arm.
- Julian watches a parent and child practicing swordsmanship and initially rebuffs Tippi’s questions. Wallace introduces himself; Julian recognizes his reputation as a wandering swordsman. Wallace explains that bandits took his arm and eye roughly two years earlier, then urges Julian to examine personal convictions when seeking an answer to his troubles. Julian’s specific dilemma is not explained here.
- A later revisit recalls the party’s first meeting with Wallace and the delivery of his eye. Another companion remembers being rescued in the village. They try to prompt Luise’s memory, but she remains silent and Tippi concludes the attempt has not worked.
- Village dialogue changes with events: residents offer healing medicine, discuss a bridge destroyed by an extraordinary gust and its later repair, react to war and Wenzel’s destruction of Boulogne, and express either renewed hope or despair. Alternative aftermath lines thank the party for saving the village while differing in how many villagers survived.
- Practical dialogue gives directions toward the Magic Academy and Grancil, warns about cave monsters and hard-to-hit specters, recommends fire against undead, and explains saving at magic-circle save points. **Tone:** Everyday village life and comic banter mixed with mentorship, gratitude, grief, and anxiety about the wider conflict.
