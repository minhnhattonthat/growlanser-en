## s050 - Suspicion at Galashiels

- **Setting and speakers:** Galashiels, a desert oasis and border town. Speakers include Ranzak soldiers, Rolandian soldiers, General Bronson, guards, townspeople, travelers, shopkeepers, and the party.
- Ranzak soldiers stop the party, who are delivering King Alcadius’s letter, and accuse them of murdering their comrades. While their credentials are checked, an attacker appears; the party realizes he intends to frame them and moves to protect the soldiers.
- The attacker escapes. The soldiers apologize, and their commander allows the messengers through. The party concludes that the killings were intended to turn Ranzak and Rolandia against each other.
- Other passages concern Burnstein’s occupation of Galashiels. Rolandian reinforcements cannot advance: an Imperial Knight’s elite troops overwhelmed the defenders, and attacking would endanger civilians.
- Residents praise Julian’s appearance and leadership, saying ordinary life continued during the occupation. Other dialogue covers restored access, a strange western cave, and a suddenly quiet house outside town.
- A traveling cook can be recruited to open a restaurant in the player’s town, `{s:0010}`. Additional branches concern the party’s merchant cover stories, shopping, lodging, and travel permits.
- **Type and tone:** Main-story border diplomacy and a combat setup, mixed with town dialogue, an optional recruitment event, and routine shop/inn system text. Suspicion and wartime caution alternate with relaxed local chatter.

## s051 - Julian Backs Elliot’s Claim

- **Setting and speakers:** Burnstein-occupied Galashiels and its approaches. Principal speakers include Julian, Elliot, Tippi, Luise, Wallace, Burnstein soldiers, townspeople, and other party members.
- The party considers how to contact Julian without provoking the opposing armies. Tippi goes because her small size and ability to fly make her suitable; she returns with instructions to meet Julian in the cave south of town.
- Elliot worries whether he can govern Burnstein even if his royal identity is genuine. Player responses produce encouragement, awkward flattery, or discouragement; Wallace emphasizes honest government and concern for others.
- After Ranzak agrees to let the troops withdraw, Julian introduces Elliot to the officers as the rightful king and declares the reigning Richard an impostor. Julian’s father, Lord Douglas, is preparing to approach the capital with Angela.
- Elliot asks for help stopping Gevel’s domination. Julian warns that they must fight their own countrymen and will be condemned as rebels if defeated, then permits anyone unwilling to leave. The soldiers remain out of trust in Julian.
- Their loyalty has personal costs: soldiers discuss endangered families and fighting friends. One plans to urge his family to seek refuge in Douglas’s territory. Civilians also credit Julian with keeping the occupation orderly.
- Julian asks Elliot to accompany the unit to strengthen morale and promises to protect him with his life. Elliot leaves the party, and his equipment is recovered. The party discusses reporting to Alcadius and helping Douglas; another system message awards “Tippi’s Potential 4.”
- **Type and tone:** Major main-story political and party-composition event, with optional responses and ordinary town services. Earnest, inspiring, and conscious of civil war’s costs, with comic exchanges around Tippi and Elliot’s uncertainty.

## s052 - Ranzak’s Capital and the Destroyed Castle

- **Setting and speakers:** Ranzak’s capital, castle gates, and the site of the destroyed castle. Speakers include Weber, Wallace, Wenzel, Elliot, Luise, Ariost, a Featherian messenger, guards, merchants, citizens, and other companions.
- Several visits are represented. The party presents Alcadius’s letters requesting an alliance or warning about Gevel and gains an audience. Other passages involve waiting for Weber or arriving by teleport ahead of approaching Burnstein forces.
- Wallace introduces Elliot to Weber, who initially mistakes him for Richard. The party requests that Ranzak refrain from pursuing Julian’s withdrawing army. Wenzel promises to recommend this to the king, stating that he wants to prevent Gevel’s rule over humanity.
- In another sequence, a citizen reports that a man who looked like a magician killed a guard through extraordinary physical strength. Weber pursued him toward the northwestern forest; the party identifies the man as Wenzel.
- The party feels an enormous magical shock and subsequently finds the castle gone. Weber says the king and many subordinates were inside, condemns Wenzel for betraying the shelter granted in exchange for teaching magic, and rushes away.
- The companions connect the destruction to a single magical discharge. Luise says even releasing all her Grosh could not accomplish this. Their reactions stress shock, helplessness, and the difficulty of confronting such power.
- A Featherian messenger summons the party on the queen’s orders and specifically requires Ariost to attend. Branches account for his presence or absence. Town dialogue also describes Weber as a powerful, kindly former mercenary and shows shortages, fear, and preparations to flee.
- **Type and tone:** Main-story diplomacy, pursuit, catastrophe, and a new summons, interspersed with shops, lodging, and access messages. The tone shifts from everyday wartime unease to devastation and urgency.

## s053 - Elliot’s Identity and Gevel’s Scheme

- **Setting and speakers:** Ranzak’s royal castle, including the audience chamber and a private room. Principal speakers are the Ranzak king, Weber, Wallace, Wenzel, Elliot, Tippi, Luise, and other companions. Sandra receives the conversation through Tippi.
- The king accepts a mutual nonaggression alliance with Rolandia and supplies a reply for Alcadius. Wallace reunites with Weber: he lost both eyes and his dominant arm while searching for their commander, and the hero’s mother made his prostheses. Weber claims not to have seen Gamlan for nearly twenty years; a companion contradicts this, and Wallace attributes the concealment to politically sensitive dealings with Burnstein.
- Wenzel identifies himself as Burnstein’s former court magician and Sandra’s teacher. He had sent information about Elliot’s origins with Elliot’s foster parent, but attackers stole the letter. He asks Tippi, explicitly identified as a homunculus, to let Sandra witness his explanation.
- According to Wenzel, ancient Growsians created Gevel to dominate humanity, while another Growsian sealed him away. Gevel emerged eighteen years ago and still follows his original purpose. Unable to acquire magic himself, he sought control over magic users and human society.
- Wenzel reveals that **Elliot is the original Prince Richard**. Gevel created a replacement from the prince’s biological information; Wenzel admits obtaining the cells and exchanging the babies under coercion. He secretly saved Elliot and arranged his upbringing and education as a means of thwarting Gevel.
- The duplicate was enhanced and is linked telepathically to Gevel; Wenzel connects this to Richard’s exceptional strength and leadership of the Imperial Knights at fourteen. Gevel also produced protective soldiers, identified in the discussion with the masked men who mutilated Wallace. After the impostor’s coronation, Wenzel sought refuge in Ranzak by offering magical instruction.
- Elliot’s magical royal bracelet can establish his identity. Wenzel directs him to his mother, Angela, confined in a royal villa east of Garuaos Prison, supplies a letter, and promises to testify. He further says Gevel loses his powers before Growsians, ordered their extermination, and used Jung and the Shadow Knights; Maxwell exploited those killings for his own abductions. Wenzel founded the Shadow Knights and was their first master, but identifies Gamlan only as their **probable** current leader.
- **Type and tone:** Central main-story revelation and exposition, framed by diplomacy and reunion. Wenzel says he protected Luise because her exceptional Growsian power makes her uniquely capable of defeating Gevel. The tone combines shock, gratitude, guarded admissions, and renewed resolve.

## s054 - Crane Village and the Growsian Murders

- **Setting and speakers:** Crane Village, Zemekis’s house, and the route toward the northern waterfall. Speakers include Zemekis, Julian, Wallace, Luise, Tippi, Xenos, villagers, children, soldiers, a monster-tamer apprentice, and gremlins.
- An apprentice demonstrates that the village gremlins are controlled by scented powders rather than domesticated. Mixing powders, judging wind, understanding monster behavior, and controlling dosage allow skilled tamers to direct monsters, even from a distance.
- Wallace revisits Chief Zemekis, who helped him about two years earlier, before the waterfall expedition on which he was attacked. Zemekis says people approaching the waterfall have since disappeared, including his son. The surrounding land formerly belonged to Wenzel and became royal property after his disappearance.
- Zemekis recognizes Luise as a Growsian; the party specifies that she was born during a total solar eclipse. He suspects his own Grosh explains why he returned safely from the waterfall, permits the investigation, and warns Luise about Growsian disappearances and suspicious deaths dating from Richard’s coronation.
- Later passages reveal Zemekis’s murder. Julian initially suspects the party, then explains that Zemekis was Burnstein’s last remaining Growsian. Witnesses describe a fleeing, heavily built man in pale armor with golden gauntlets; these passages do not name the killer.
- The party explains its investigation of Gevel and the masked men. They report defeating creatures and masked attackers in the waterfall cave, and a soldier independently confirms the bodies. Julian accepts their account, takes charge of further investigation, and lets them leave to avoid harming the village, while warning that they remain enemies on the battlefield.
- Optional material includes returning a keepsake knife associated with Zemekis’s son and receiving Zemekis’s staff, obtaining “Tippi’s Potential 2,” and Xenos thinking of Karen before urging action against Gamlan. Separate dialogue has Xenos leave on unspecified business. Residents express gratitude for safer access to the waterfall and grief for their chief.
- **Type and tone:** Main-story investigation and murder aftermath, with side rewards, a monster-taming demonstration, and routine shop/inn messages. Initial curiosity and warmth give way to grief, suspicion, and uneasy cooperation across enemy lines.
