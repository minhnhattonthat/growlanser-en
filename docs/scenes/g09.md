## s037 - Misha’s Missing Home

- **Setting and speakers:** Medis village, Bradley’s mansion and hidden basement, and the place Misha remembers as her childhood home. Speakers include Misha, Tippi, Luise, Bradley, Karen, other party members, villagers, a servant, a shopkeeper, and a doctor.
- Misha repeatedly disappears while the party visits the village. She invites the hero home, describing a solitary house above terraced flowerbeds. She says her parents died when she was little and the Academy headmaster took her in.
- Luise worries about her best friend’s contradictory memories and behavior: Misha disappears in Medis, does not recognize the vice-headmaster despite supposedly sharing his village, and previously spoke strangely on Mount Bream. Player responses address whether to trust, question, or help her.
- Misha finds neither her remembered flowerbeds nor her red-roofed house. Karen says the area has always looked this way. Misha recognizes that no house appears to have stood there, yet vividly remembers growing up there with her parents.
- Misha questions her identity and whether her memories—or her entire existence—are artificial. Branching responses offer an embrace, patience, encouragement, or silence; some passages calm her, while others leave her distressed and running away. Luise affirms their friendship, and the party resolves to investigate.
- A separate investigation concerns the missing Growsians and Irene. Bradley rushes to the Academy when told about the disappearance. The party later enters his unlocked mansion, finds a concealed switch in a painting, and opens stairs to a basement.
- The basement contains magical research equipment and a circle that prevents magical effects from escaping outside, but no Growsians or relevant research. The party reviews unresolved clues: a suspicious person carrying a box, Bradley’s acquaintance found dead in the hall, and a red earring in the dead person’s hand.
- **Type and tone:** Main-story investigation mixed with Misha’s character event, village conversations, shops, inn services, and an Injector upgrade hint. Comic banter and nervous trespassing give way to an intense identity crisis. No evident debug text or battle sequence.

## s038 - Rachelle’s Flowers and Berger’s Farewell

- **Setting and speakers:** Rachelle, including its flower garden, streets, inn, and the area outside the sanatorium. Major speakers include Misha, Tippi, Luise, Ariost, Wenzel, Berger, Wallace, Karen, and Xenos, alongside townspeople and staff. The file combines several visits, branches, and events.
- In a lighthearted flower-garden event, Misha makes the hero a flower crown. She recalls the headmaster comforting her with *kurumayuri* lilies after her parents died, identifies their flower meaning as “a person of many talents,” and invites the hero to see her remembered flowerbeds in Medis.
- Another garden encounter follows Misha’s memory crisis. Luise asks her not to flee or suffer alone; the hero and companions offer reassurance. Misha agrees to take more time to think, emphasizing her friendship with Luise and the party’s support.
- Beautiful singing reportedly comes from the eastern forest, where the Featherian queen is said to be. Ariost asks Luise to help him visit Featherland immediately to determine whether the voice belongs to his mother. The party discusses accompanying him while also seeking an antidote for the hero’s master.
- In a major confrontation, Wenzel attacks the weakened hero. The player chooses whom to shield from among the available companions; the hero is incapacitated but can still give battle orders. Berger asks Wallace and the others to hold Wenzel off while he reaches the hero. Later dialogue has Wenzel escape after claiming that the world will perish and only he can save it.
- Dying Berger reveals that he led a rebellion when Growsians ruled over people living like slaves. Wenzel captured and experimented on him, then used those results to fuse with Gevel. Berger escaped with a Power Stone and found himself in the present era; he believes the stone carried him through time.
- Berger also reveals that Karen is his stepdaughter. After Sierra’s death, he raised Xenos and later married Karen’s mother, his close friend’s wife; Karen and Xenos are therefore not biological siblings. Berger dies urging the party to defeat Wenzel. Karen leaves the party, and subsequent dialogue directs attention to preventing Wenzel’s teleportation and contacting Elliot in Burnstein.
- **Type and tone:** Main story and battle dialogue mixed with character side events, shopping, lodging, and local activities. Optional material includes hiring a theater troupe for 800 Elm and Pechi waiting for his hospitalized owner, with dialogue reflecting her recovery or death. The tone ranges from flirtatious comedy and reassurance to warfare, revelations, and bereavement; no evident debug text.

## s039 - Patients and Missing Growsians

- **Setting and speakers:** Rachelle’s sanatorium, including wards and staff rooms. Speakers include Karen, Irene, Tippi, Luise, Misha, Ariost, Alto, doctors, nurses, the head nurse, and patients, including an unnamed girl and a man with amnesia.
- The party reunites with Karen, who was brought here injured and now helps the staff. Irene treated her. Learning that both Irene and Luise are Growsians prompts Karen to reconsider her assumptions: their greater magical power does not make them fundamentally different from other people.
- Karen confirms hearing beautiful singing from the east; other dialogue connects the forest with ancient Growsian ruins. Optional recruitment passages let Karen join the party, require choosing a replacement when necessary, and acknowledge a herbal medicine book the hero sent through her brother.
- The party searches for Irene because Growsians are being targeted. Soldiers have already searched unsuccessfully. Staff say her absence is unusually long and suggest asking her lover, Nick, who volunteered for frontier duty to stay near her and lives in Medis. Xenos has also asked about her.
- Patient Alto describes his romance with Mira, a childhood friend he met again in Grancil after ten years apart. Formerly a nurse here, she moved to Burnstein to oversee medical affairs. Dialogue progresses from affectionate letters to anxiety, reassurance that Growsians are protected at the Academy, and shock when they disappear.
- Later passages report that the Academy headmaster was responsible and Mira has died. Alto tells the party not to blame themselves, thanks them for helping her and saving other Growsians, and urges them to continue their work. These are distinct stages of his story, not one continuous conversation.
- An isolated young patient says her parents fight over her treatment costs and only her dog Pechi understands her. Visits, flowers, and GL Chips collectible cards can encourage her to recover and help reconcile her parents. Other dialogue records alternative outcomes: she returns home recovered or dies. Staff also discuss a longstanding amnesiac patient whose hair turned white despite his comparatively young age.
- **Type and tone:** Main-story leads mixed with recruitment menus and substantial patient side stories. Warm reunions, teasing romance, and playful collectible cards coexist with prejudice reconsidered, loneliness, medical care, and grief. No evident debug or battle text.
