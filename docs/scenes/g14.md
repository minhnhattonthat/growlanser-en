## s061 - Burnstein’s crisis and the Power Stone

- **Where/type:** Burnstein Castle, including its audience chamber, dungeon, and service rooms. Mainly main-story events across several story states, with incidental NPC dialogue and party-management/system prompts.
- **Speakers:** The party, King Elliot, Imperial Knights Ernest/Lyell and Oscar/Reeves, soldiers, citizens, and castle staff. Queen Mother Angela is listed. Many speaker names remain represented by tokens.
- The party reports Ranzak Castle’s instantaneous destruction and summons the Imperial Knights. Another exchange brings Ariost into the party because the Featherians specifically request his presence; he wonders whether the summons concerns his mother.
- Strategy discussions establish that Wenzel is fused with Gevel and can manipulate space-time. The Power Stone has removed his extraordinary regeneration, making him vulnerable, but he can still escape through teleportation. His smaller Gevel-like creatures have attacked the Magic Academy.
- Wenzel threatens indiscriminate killing and demands the Power Stone at Boulogne, Ariost’s village. The group debates sacrificing its strongest resource to save hostages; although the king initially refuses, frightened citizens demand submission, prompting a public promise to surrender it.
- Lyell volunteers to deliver the stone, citing the king’s command to protect people and his wish to restore his honor. Subsequent dialogue has Wenzel reject his promise and announce the village’s destruction. The party responds with anger, guilt, and concern for Ernest and the villagers.
- Other passages reveal Richard’s disappearance from the dungeon, a dead guard, and Ernest/Lyell’s absence. Ernest confronts Oscar: he prioritizes remaining with Richard and has orders to kill anyone he cannot recruit. He rejects cooperation when the party cannot guarantee Richard’s survival.
- **Reveals/tone:** The hero and Richard could use their abilities to make people Growsians, enabling another Power Stone to operate the space-time control device and prevent the world’s collapse. Public panic, divided loyalties, and betrayal dominate, offset by reports that the party’s efforts are restoring people’s hope.

## s063 - Featherland’s refusal and a queen’s rescue

- **Where/type:** Featherland, including its assembly area and “reflection room.” Main-story visits mixed with NPC hostility, choices, an item reward, and travel/system guidance.
- **Speakers:** Ariost, Luise, the party, the Featherian queen, and unnamed Featherians. Gina is discussed but remains inaccessible.
- The party seeks medicine for Luise’s poisoned mother, also addressed as “Master.” The Featherians condemn human selfishness, warfare, and possible misuse of Ariost’s flying device; they say human conflicts forced them to abandon the surface.
- The queen offers one dose of antidote if the visitors demonstrate even one respect in which humans surpass Featherians. The party decides to study Featherian thinking, while Luise fears for her mother.
- On another visit, the party learns that a human magician abducted the queen. The Featherian assembly refuses a rescue because the expected casualties outweigh saving one replaceable ruler.
- Ariost reveals that he is half Featherian and came to find his mother, Gina. She has spent twenty years in the reflection room because she lived with a human and refuses to admit that this was wrong; he is denied a meeting.
- The party later returns with the rescued queen and receives Featherian medicine. She acknowledges the value of refusing to give up before trying, but still withholds access to Gina, promising her freedom if human superiority is demonstrated. Ariost becomes an accompanying character.
- **Tone:** Open prejudice and cold calculations clash with compassion, anger, and filial longing. The rescue earns limited personal respect, rather than general acceptance of humanity.

## s064 - Wenzel’s origins and Gina’s answer

- **Where/type:** Featherland’s royal audience and Gina’s confinement room, with recollections of the surface village where she met Ariost’s father. Mainly main-story exposition and quest preparation, plus shop and travel dialogue.
- **Speakers:** Queen Stella, Gina, Ariost, Luise, Tippi, the hero’s companions, unnamed Featherians, and the original Sukimaya shopkeeper. Flashback speakers include Ariost’s unnamed father and children he supports.
- Stella identifies Wenzel as the last ancient Growsian king. Seeking immortality and supreme power, he researched fusion with Gevel. She identifies him as responsible for the ancient Growsian massacre; the suggestion that he lost control during an experiment is presented as the characters’ explanation, not a witnessed fact.
- Featherians and humans originally came from another world, whereas Gevel originated here. Wenzel’s status as a total-eclipse Growsian, combined with fusion and Luise’s stolen Grosh, explains his exceptional space-time powers. Ancient Featherian science and human magic created the device that overlaps the worlds; Wenzel now possesses it.
- The hero experiences recalled statements about Gevel’s creations depending on their creator for life, then regains consciousness. Tippi blames herself for making him attend a party, while Luise connects his condition to Gevel’s death; his companions urge him to disclose any danger.
- Gina reunites with Ariost and identifies the hero’s ring as a Power Stone: concentrated Grosh and human will, originally made for the space-time device. Its miracles manipulate causality and incur an unpredictable later cost. A separate controller can direct its effects toward Wenzel, but cannot eliminate the resulting backlash.
- Retrieving that controller requires entering a monster-filled artificial cave without Grosh. Since its sealed door requires magic, the party must recruit a mana-line engineer from the Magic Academy. Gina later explains that everyone must strongly share the same wish while the stone’s bearer activates the controller near the target.
- **Relationships/tone:** Gina’s account shows how Ariost’s father healed her injured wing and cared for orphaned children, changing her contempt into love and admiration for human perseverance. Featherians forcibly separated them. Warm family reunion and hope coexist with grave revelations, the hero’s vulnerability, and concern about the Power Stone’s cost.

## s065 - A shared burden to save the world

- **Where/type:** Featherland, including conversations with Gina. Main-story planning and follow-up dialogue, alongside ambient NPC remarks, shopping, and travel/system text.
- **Speakers:** Gina, Ariost, Luise, Tippi, Wallace, the player-named hero through choices, Queen Stella, unnamed Featherians, and the original Sukimaya shopkeeper.
- Gina has left confinement, and a Featherian admits their judgment was mistaken. Residents show growing curiosity about humans and their refusal to give up, although some remain brusque or skeptical.
- The party reports that the hero can turn ordinary people into Growsians; the Academy headmaster has already undergone the change. Each use increases space-time distortion, and the hero can probably endure only one or two more. Gevel’s death has left him with ordinary human stamina.
- Gina proposes using the Power Stone production apparatus’s telepathic function—the same system Wenzel uses to address people—to reach many people at once. This might create enough Growsians for a new Power Stone, but the exertion could kill the hero. His companions reject treating his life as expendable.
- Another being with the same abilities could share the burden. The party identifies Richard, Gevel’s duplicate of Elliot, believed to be imprisoned beneath Burnstein Castle. Gina says their combined effort could enable mass transformation and a replacement Power Stone.
- The transformation would greatly increase distortion, so the stone must be completed and used before the worlds separate. A later visit reveals that Richard serves Wenzel. Gina leaves open the possibility of the hero succeeding alone, but offers no guarantee.
- **Stakes/tone:** The party believes Wenzel would separate the worlds as he dies, so it must produce the stone while enduring his attacks and also solve his teleportation advantage. Growing reconciliation and renewed hope are tempered by the risk to the hero and unresolved tactical problems.
